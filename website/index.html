1 · About you

Your madhhab affects a few specific rules (e.g. whether a grandfather shares with siblings, and how shortfalls are handled). The calculation explains every share it produces.

2 · Estate & liabilities

Things a will cannot reach: property held as joint tenants passes automatically to the co-owner outside your will (a solicitor can sever it to tenants-in-common); pensions and life insurance usually pass by nomination forms — update your pension "Expression of Wish" to match your faraid shares.
Foreign assets may need a separate local will — take advice in each country.

3 · Wasiyya (bequest) — optional

You may leave up to one-third of your net estate to charity or to people who are not your Islamic heirs. The Prophet ﷺ told Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas: "A third, and a third is much" (Bukhari 2742, Muslim 1628).

Bequests to people who are already Quranic heirs classically require the other heirs' consent after death — the generated will flags any such bequest.

4 · Your family (heirs)

Siblings

Wider family (only inherit when closer heirs are absent)

Non-Muslim relatives and adopted children do not inherit by faraid — you can provide for them through the wasiyya third. This prototype assumes all listed heirs are Muslim; the full app asks per person.

5 · Executor & witnesses

Two witnesses (both present together when you sign)

Wills Act 1837, s.15: a witness (or a witness's spouse) must not be a beneficiary — otherwise that gift fails. Witnesses must be 18+ and of sound mind. Choose witnesses who inherit nothing under this will.

6 · Faraid shares

Calculations follow published classical rules of your selected school (engine v1.0.0, parity-tested against 1,235 reference cases). For complex family situations, confirm with a qualified scholar.
