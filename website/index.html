1 · About you

A will drawn under the law of England & Wales can still be made if you live abroad, but which country's law governs your estate depends on your domicile and where assets are — take advice from a solicitor familiar with cross-border estates. Foreign assets may need a local will.

2 · Estate & liabilities

Things a will cannot reach: joint-tenancy property passes to the co-owner outside your will (a solicitor can sever to tenants-in-common); pensions and life insurance usually pass by nomination forms — update your pension "Expression of Wish" to match your faraid shares.

3 · Wasiyya (bequest) — optional

You may leave up to one-third of your net estate to charity or to people who are not your Islamic heirs. "A third, and a third is much" (Bukhari 2742, Muslim 1628). If you leave nothing here, the bequest clause is simply left out of your will.

5 · Executor, witnesses & guardian

Two witnesses (both present together when you sign)

Wills Act 1837, s.15: a witness (or a witness's spouse) must not be a beneficiary — otherwise that gift fails. Witnesses must be 18+ and of sound mind.

6 · Faraid shares

Calculations follow published classical rules of your selected school (engine v1.0.0, parity-tested against 1,235 reference cases). For complex family situations, confirm with a qualified scholar.

7 · Your will (review)

You can edit names, executor, assets etc. — the shares themselves stay as calculated.

8 · Download, sign & upload

1. Download & print. Print your will single-sided and initial each page.

2. Sign. Sign the attestation page with both witnesses present together; they then sign in front of you (Wills Act 1837 s.9).

3. Upload the signed copy (photos of each page — kept in your account).

Keep the original paper will somewhere safe and tell your executor where it is. The upload is a convenience copy, not the legal original.
